# PiCoCo Cartridge Breakout v1.0

A dumb adapter: the 40 fingers of a Tandy Color Computer cartridge slot
brought out 1:1 to a 2x20 0.1" header, so the PiCoCo bus interface can
be breadboarded before the real board is routed. See
`docs/breadboard-plan.md` for why and for the bring-up sequence.

```
CoCo slot ──[this board]──40-way IDC ribbon──[2x20-to-breadboard adapter]──breadboard
```

Everything here is generated by `tools/gen_breakout.py` (run it with
KiCad's bundled Python). Do not hand-edit the `.kicad_sch`; the `.kicad_pcb`
is also generated, including all routing, and is safe to regenerate.

## What is on the board

| Ref | Part | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | 40 gold fingers (`PiCoCo:COCO-CART-FINGERS`) | mates with the CoCo slot |
| J1 | 2x20 shrouded (keyed) IDC box header, vertical | header pin n = cart pin n; the key stops a reversed ribbon |
| F1 | 0.5 A hold polyfuse, 1812 | in series with +5V so a breadboard slip cannot short the CoCo's 5 V rail |
| C1 | 10 uF 0805 X5R, >= 10 V | +5V bulk after the fuse |
| C2 | 100 nF 0805 | +5V bypass |
| R1 + D1 | 1 kΩ 0805 + 0805 LED | power indicator |

Cart fingers 1 and 2 (-12 V and +12 V on CoCo 1/2, unused on CoCo 3) are
**left open**, so ±12 V can never reach the ribbon. Header pins 1 and 2
are tied to GND instead, which gives the ribbon four ground conductors
(1, 2, 33, 34) rather than two.

Board: 52.8 x 99 mm, 2 layer, 1.6 mm. Measured on the CoCo: from the
connector face to the outside of the case is about 1 3/4" (44.5 mm), so
with the 10 mm finger tab the case surface sits about 55 mm from the
finger tip. The header's near row is at 77 mm, its shroud starts at
about 74 mm, so there is a 17 mm bare grip zone outside the case below
the shroud, and the ribbon plugs in with the case on. A silk line at
55 mm marks the approximate case surface. Both copper layers carry a
GND pour that stops 1 mm short of the finger tab.

The shroud of J1 is 58.4 mm long and the board is 52.8 mm wide, so the
plastic overhangs each side edge by about 2.8 mm. That is deliberate:
a keyed header is worth more than a flush one. (An earlier note here
claimed the board could not be wider than the finger tab; measured on
2026-09-18 the CoCo 3 opening is 114.3 x 30.2 mm, so it can.) A
reversed ribbon at an unkeyed header would land +5V on /CTS.

### Finger geometry versus the original cart footprint

Finger width (1.27 mm), pitch (2.54 mm), x positions, layer assignment
(odd on top, even on bottom), and the far end of every pad are identical
to `COCO-CART-2.1X1.75`, the footprint the main PiCoCo board uses. One
thing was changed on purpose: the leading edge of the 39 full-length
fingers was pulled back from 0.44 mm to 1.30 mm from the board edge.
JLCPCB's order form states that a 30° bevel on 1.6 mm stock is 1.13 mm
deep (0.53 mm residual thickness at the tip), so any copper closer than
that sits on the chamfer and gets ground off, leaving bare copper at
the finger tips. 1.30 mm keeps every finger fully on the flat. The
contact wipe zone is millimetres further in, so mating is unaffected.
The +5V finger (9) was already short at the leading edge (mates last)
and is unchanged. The main board's footprint still has the 0.44 mm
figure and should get the same trim before it is fabbed.

Off-board, for the breadboard end:

- 40-way IDC ribbon, 15-20 cm, with 2x20 female IDC sockets on both ends
  (sold as "40-pin Raspberry Pi GPIO ribbon cable"). Keep it short; the
  CoCo bus is unterminated.
- The `PiCoCo-Cobbler` board from this directory plus one 2x20 shrouded
  box header and two 1x20 pin headers. Do not substitute a Raspberry Pi
  cobbler; see the note below.

`fab/breakout/PiCoCo-Breakout-BOM.csv` and `-pos.csv` are the machine
readable versions, if you want JLCPCB to place the five SMD parts.

If you would rather not solder SMD: F1 can be replaced by a wire link
(you lose the short-circuit protection), C1/C2/R1/D1 can all be left
off (the board still works, you lose the LED and local bypass).
Everything else is through-hole.

## Ordering

Gerbers, drill and position files are in `fab/breakout/`;
`PiCoCo-Breakout-gerbers.zip` is the upload. Regenerate with:

```
bash tools/gen_fab.sh breakout/PiCoCo-Breakout.kicad_pcb fab/breakout
```

Use the JLCPCB settings in `fab/READ-BEFORE-ORDERING.txt`. The ones that
matter for this board, as the JLCPCB form presents them:

- Layers 2, thickness 1.6 mm (slot fit), 1 oz copper.
- **Surface finish: ENIG.** JLCPCB only offers the gold-finger process
  on an ENIG board; with HASL selected the form warns that the fingers
  will merely be tinned.
- **Gold Fingers: Yes. Beveling: 30°.** The form then shows the bevel
  depth (1.13 mm) and residual thickness (0.53 mm) the finger geometry
  was designed around.
- Confirm Production File: Yes is worth the extra day on a gold-finger
  board; you get to see how they interpreted the fingers and bevel.
- Everything else at defaults: outline tolerance ±0.2 mm, flying-probe
  test, no castellations, no edge plating, no blind slots.
- Minimum quantity is 5. Keep the spares.

Fab-rule check against the published requirements (JLCPCB help
"Gold fingers"; PCBWay "PCB Gold fingers" page):

| Rule | Requirement | This board |
|---|---|---|
| finger to board edge, 30° bevel, 1.6 mm | JLC bevel depth 1.13 mm (order form); help page says >= 0.6 mm; PCBWay >= 0.5 mm | 1.30 mm |
| copper in the bevel zone | none | none: fingers end at 1.30 mm, pours stop 11 mm up, traces leave pads inward |
| pads / plated holes near fingers | >= 1.0 mm | nearest is J1 at ~38 mm |
| board size after bevel | JLC >= 50 x 50 mm, PCBWay width > 45 mm | 52.8 x 99 mm (inside the 100 x 100 mm price tier) |
| fingers on one edge only | yes | yes |

On PCBWay's order form the equivalents are "Gold fingers: Yes",
"Beveling: 30°", surface finish for the fingers is implied by the gold
finger option. The cobbler has no fingers and needs no special options.

When the boards arrive, test-fit one into the slot with the CoCo **off**
before soldering anything.

## The cobbler note (read before buying an adapter)

Raspberry Pi "cobbler" style adapters are the obvious 2x20-IDC-to-
breadboard part, and their silkscreen will show Pi names (GPIO2, 5V,
GND...) that mean nothing here; ribbon conductor n still lands on
breadboard position n. The Pi header pin names, for cross-reference when
you are staring at a cobbler:

| Pin | Pi label | Pin | Pi label |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3V3 | 2 | 5V |
| 3 | GPIO2 | 4 | 5V |
| 5 | GPIO3 | 6 | GND |
| 7 | GPIO4 | 8 | GPIO14 |
| 9 | GND | 10 | GPIO15 |
| 11 | GPIO17 | 12 | GPIO18 |
| 13 | GPIO27 | 14 | GND |
| 15 | GPIO22 | 16 | GPIO23 |
| 17 | 3V3 | 18 | GPIO24 |
| 19 | GPIO10 | 20 | GND |
| 21 | GPIO9 | 22 | GPIO25 |
| 23 | GPIO11 | 24 | GPIO8 |
| 25 | GND | 26 | GPIO7 |
| 27 | ID_SD | 28 | ID_SC |
| 29 | GPIO5 | 30 | GND |
| 31 | GPIO6 | 32 | GPIO12 |
| 33 | GPIO13 | 34 | GND |
| 35 | GPIO19 | 36 | GPIO16 |
| 37 | GPIO26 | 38 | GPIO20 |
| 39 | GND | 40 | GPIO21 |

**The trap, and it is real:** Adafruit's T-Cobbler Plus joins all eight
GND-labelled pins (6, 9, 14, 20, 25, 30, 34, 39) into one net and both
5V pins (2, 4) into another, on the cobbler PCB itself. That is verified
from the Eagle schematic in Adafruit's `Adafruit-Pi-Cobber-PCBs` GitHub
repo. Plugged into this breakout it would short CoCo E, +5V, D4, A1, A6,
A11, GND and A15 together the moment the ribbon is seated. Assume every
Pi cobbler and Pi GPIO breakout does the same unless you have measured
it.

**Use `PiCoCo-Cobbler` instead.** It is the second board in this
directory: a keyed 2x20 IDC box header on top, two 1x20 pin rows
underneath 0.3" apart to straddle the breadboard channel, and forty
straight traces. Nothing is bussed. The shroud key means the ribbon can
only seat one way at that end. Order it in the same JLCPCB batch
(`fab/cobbler/PiCoCo-Cobbler-gerbers.zip`, plain 2-layer, no gold
fingers needed). Parts: one 2x20 shrouded box header, two 1x20 pin
headers, soldered with the pin headers on the bottom.

## Assembly notes

- Cobbler: solder the two 1x20 pin headers on the **bottom** (long pins
  down) and the box header on top. Trim the box header's pin stubs flush
  on the bottom; they sit over the breadboard's centre channel, and on
  breadboards where that channel is not a groove the stubs would rest on
  the plastic.
- Cobbler: the two pin rows are 0.3" apart, so they land in rows e and f
  of a standard breadboard, leaving four free holes per signal on each
  side.
- Breakout: orientation is the same as a Program Pak: component side
  (the side with the "1" finger label and all the parts) faces up in a
  CoCo 3 side slot, i.e. the way the cartridge label would face.
- Ribbon: 40-way, female IDC both ends, red stripe to pin 1 at both
  ends. Shorter is better; 15-20 cm is plenty. The CoCo bus has 5 ns
  TTL edges and only four ground returns in the ribbon, so a 50 cm
  cable will ring.

## Before the first insertion (five minutes with a meter)

1. Breakout, nothing plugged in: no continuity between header pin 9
   (+5V) and any of pins 33/34/1/2 (GND); no continuity between any two
   adjacent header pins except 1-2 and 33-34.
2. Continuity finger 3 to header pin 3, finger 10 to pin 10, finger 39
   to pin 39, finger 40 to pin 40 (spot-check both sides and both rows).
3. Continuity finger 9 to header pin 9 through F1 (a few ohms).
4. Ribbon plus cobbler plugged in: continuity from finger 9 to the
   cobbler pin labelled +5V, and from finger 33 to the cobbler GND pin.
   If +5V lands on /CTS, a ribbon end is reversed.

## Bring-up checks (from docs/breadboard-plan.md, steps 1-2)

1. Board alone in the slot, ribbon unplugged: CoCo boots to BASIC, D1
   lights, +5V reads about 5 V at header pin 9.
2. Logic analyzer on E, Q, R/W, /CTS, /SCS at the breadboard:
   `PEEK(&HC000)` pulses /CTS, `PEEK(&HFF41)` pulses /SCS.

Power the CoCo off before inserting or removing the board. The fuse
protects the CoCo's rail from shorts, not the CoCo from hot-plugging.
